## Independent binary model adopted for node selection

Node selection was revised to use the same hypothesis structure as edge
characterization. For each candidate protein (i), the mutually exclusive
hypotheses are:

- (H_{1,i}): the protein is present in the signaling system; and
- (H_{0,i}): the protein is not present in the signaling system.

The default objective prior assigns equal probability to the two hypotheses:

$$
P(H_{1,i})=P(H_{0,i})=0.5.
$$

Each protein is a separate Bernoulli hypothesis. The posterior probabilities
are not normalized across the 9,170 candidates and are not required to sum to
one. Consequently, evidence supporting one protein does not reduce the
probability of another protein merely by reallocating a fixed amount of
probability mass.

Protein-abundance and principal-cell RNA source scores retain their historical
range from 0.5 to 1. Before integration, each is divided by its neutral value
0.5:

$$
BF_{i,s}=f_{i,s}/0.5.
$$

The neutral source score therefore becomes (BF=1), and the strongest possible
score approaches (BF=2). Kinase-activity and phosphoprotein-response tables
already store neutral-relative multipliers with (BF=1) as the neutral value.
Missing or inapplicable evidence is also assigned (BF=1).

For enabled stream (s) with user weight (w_s):

$$
O_i^{\mathrm{post}}
=O_i^{\mathrm{prior}}
\prod_s BF_{i,s}^{w_s},
\qquad
P_i^{\mathrm{post}}
=\frac{O_i^{\mathrm{post}}}{1+O_i^{\mathrm{post}}}.
$$

At the default prior of 0.5, prior odds equal one. A protein receiving only
neutral or missing evidence remains exactly at posterior 0.5. The default
selection rule retains proteins whose posterior is strictly greater than 0.5.
The GUI now exposes both the node prior and the selection cutoff.

The previous sum-to-one operation was appropriate only for a categorical model
in which exactly one candidate was assumed to be the relevant node. That model
does not match a signaling network, where many proteins may simultaneously be
present. It was therefore removed from current node inference. Source-score
normalization to a neutral (BF=1) remains necessary; cross-node posterior
normalization does not.
